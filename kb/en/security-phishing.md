# Recognizing phishing messages

Phishing is an attempt to trick you into revealing sensitive information by pretending to be a trusted sender. Attackers may imitate kremzaPay in emails, SMS messages, or fake websites. Knowing what kremzaPay will never ask for is your best defense.

## What kremzaPay will never do

- Ask for your password, `api_key`, or full card number by email or SMS.
- Ask you to change your payout account by email.
- Send you a link and ask you to "verify" your login through it.

Genuine kremzaPay support may ask for your `merchant_id` and identifiers such as a `sessionId` or payout id, but never for secrets that grant account access.

## How to check a message

1. **Check the sender domain.** Look at the full email address, not just the display name. Legitimate kremzaPay messages come from the kremzapay.demo domain. Small misspellings or extra words are a warning sign.
2. **Do not click links to log in.** Always open the kremzaPay Panel by typing the address into your browser by hand, not by following a link in a message.
3. **Be cautious of urgency.** Messages that pressure you to act immediately, threaten account suspension, or demand payout-account changes are common phishing tactics.
4. **Inspect attachments.** Do not open unexpected attachments claiming to be invoices or security notices.

## If you suspect phishing

Do not reply, click, or enter any details. Forward the suspicious message to pomoc@kremzapay.demo and then delete it. If you believe you already entered your credentials, change your password immediately and contact support through the Panel.

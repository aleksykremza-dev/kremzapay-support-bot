# Chargeback vs refund

A refund and a chargeback both return money to a customer, but they are very different processes with very different consequences for you.

**A refund is merchant-initiated.** You decide to return the payment, in full or in part, through the **Refunds** section of the Panel. There are no dispute costs, the transaction closes cleanly, and — most importantly — you keep the customer relationship. Refunds are available for 180 days after the payment, which covers most return and cancellation cases.

**A chargeback is bank-forced.** The customer goes to their card issuer, which reverses the payment for them. This carries a dispute fee, charged per your agreement, and each chargeback counts toward your chargeback rate. A rising rate increases costs and can put your account under review. You may contest a chargeback, but even a successful defence takes weeks of work.

The practical rule is simple: **when a customer asks for their money back, a refund is almost always the better path.** It is cheaper, faster, and leaves no mark on your dispute rate. Issuing the refund yourself removes the customer's reason to contact their bank at all.

| | Refund | Chargeback |
|---|---|---|
| Started by | You | The customer's bank |
| Cost | None | Dispute fee, per agreement |
| Rate impact | None | Counts against you |
| Relationship | Preserved | Usually lost |

Watch for refund requests in your support channels and act on them before they escalate. A refund handled in a day prevents a dispute that could take two months to resolve.

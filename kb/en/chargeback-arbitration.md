# Arbitration — the final stage

Arbitration is the last step in a dispute. It happens only when the issuing bank and the merchant still disagree after representment — that is, after you have already answered the dispute with evidence and the issuer has rejected it.

At this point neither side will concede, so the **card scheme** (the network behind the card) steps in and makes a binding ruling. It reviews the evidence from both parties and decides who is right. You do not negotiate directly with the bank; you present your case and wait.

Two things make arbitration different from earlier stages:

1. **Extra scheme costs apply.** Beyond the amounts already at stake, the card scheme charges additional fees for handling arbitration, as set out in your agreement. The losing side typically bears these costs.
2. **The decision is final.** There is no further appeal. Once the scheme rules, the outcome stands and the funds move accordingly.

Because of the added costs and finality, arbitration is **rarely worth pursuing for small amounts**. The scheme fees can exceed the value of the disputed transaction, so escalating a low-value case often costs more than it recovers. Weigh the disputed amount against the potential fees before deciding to continue.

If you do proceed, make sure your evidence is complete and clearly presented — you already had your chance at representment, and arbitration is about the strength of that same record.
